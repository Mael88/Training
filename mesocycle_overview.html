Why This Cycle Looks Different

Origin: Illness + Suspected Overtraining

Mesocycle 5 ended early — Week 6 was lost entirely to gastroenteritis. Around the same period, a cervical flare-up developed, suspected to be linked to upper body overtraining on the previous 3 upper + 1 legs split. The flare-up resolved fully after one week of rest, confirming the connection was plausible.

Response: the weekly split changes to 2 upper + 2 legs + 2 bag, redistributing load away from repeated upper body sessions. One re-entry bag session (RPE 5, mechanics only) has already been completed — everything checked out structurally sound.

Threads Carried From Mesocycle 5

Rear Mawashi Geri Recovery

Correction drill continues: after impact, actively pull the knee back to chamber before placing the foot down, rather than letting the leg drop. Self-diagnosed correctly in Mesocycle 5 — expect another 3–5 weeks of dedicated work.

Lead Mawashi Geri Hip Strength

Stable at “moderate” quality across Mesocycle 5. Continue the 3×/week hip protocol without changes. Ballistic strength adaptation in these muscles takes longer than one cycle.

Phase Details

Weeks 1–3 — Rebuild

Upper body intensity capped (2–3 reps from failure, no max sets) to protect against the overtraining pattern suspected in the cervical flare-up. Bag work restarts near Week 3 Mesocycle 5 intensity — the toolkit doesn’t need re-teaching, just re-loading. Kick volume stays moderate for the first 2 weeks specifically because both bag days now follow a legs day.

Upper cap lifts at Week 3 if cervical status has stayed good throughout.

Weeks 4–5 — Intensification

RPE and HR targets step up. Rear mawashi recovery and lead mawashi hip corrections continue under real fatigue — this is where they get properly tested. HR recovery tracked at matched conditions for a cleaner signal than Mesocycle 5 provided.

Week 6 — Taper

Volume drops ~30%, intensity holds. Arrive at deload sharp, with both open technical threads showing measurable progress.
