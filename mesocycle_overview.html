Cycle Priorities

Priority 1 — Lead Mawashi Geri

The lead hip rotators are weaker and less mobile than the rear side. This is addressed on two fronts: dedicated hip mobility work added to every warm-up, and lead mawashi geri given focused attention in technique sessions throughout the cycle. By Week 4, the lead roundhouse should match the rear in terms of mechanical quality if not yet in power.

Corrective cue carried forward: drive the hip forward and laterally into the kick. Torso stays upright. The hip leads — the leg follows.

Priority 2 — Atemi as Jujitsu Setup Sequences

From Week 2 onward, technique sessions begin developing strikes with explicit jujitsu intent: the jab disrupts posture and sets up a cross; a front kick creates distance for a clinch entry; a hook closes range and loads the opponent for a takedown. This is not new material — it is the same strikes with a tactical frame that makes them atemi-waza rather than boxing.

Introduced gradually in technique sessions. Output sessions condition the patterns at speed.

Priority 3 — Cardiovascular Conditioning

Breathing/cardio was the primary fatigue signal by end of Mesocycle 4. This is addressed by progressively higher cardiovascular demand in output sessions — shorter rest, more rounds in the intensification phase. The goal is for RPE 8 to feel like RPE 7 by the end of the cycle.

Priority 4 — General Technique Consolidation

All combinations that felt mechanical at the end of Mesocycle 4 — particularly jab-lead hook and cross-rear hook — get continued repetition. No rush to add new strikes until the existing toolkit flows automatically under fatigue.

Phase Details

Weeks 1–2 — Accumulation A

Full toolkit re-established at low-medium intensity. Mawashi geri hip mobility work begins in every warm-up. Week 2 introduces the first atemi-as-setup sequences in technique sessions — the jab as a posture disruptor, the front kick as a distance-creator before clinch entry.

Do not try to immediately match Week 5 Mesocycle 4 intensity. Re-build from the base up.

Week 3 — Accumulation B

Combination sequences become longer and more tactically connected. Technique sessions develop the atemi-as-setup concept further — hook closing into clinch range, mae geri creating a takedown entry window. Output sessions begin pushing the cardiovascular ceiling above Mesocycle 4 baseline.

Weeks 4–5 — Intensification

RPE and HR targets reach and exceed Mesocycle 4 peaks. The full tactical sequence (strike → setup → entry point) is drilled under output conditions. Mawashi geri is pushed at higher output. This is where the cardiovascular conditioning target is stress-tested.

Breathing/cardio will be the limiter again. That is expected and correct. The goal is to push that ceiling further than Mesocycle 4.

Week 6 — Taper

Volume drops ~30%, intensity stays. Same full toolkit. Arrive at deload sharp and consolidated.

Week 7 — Deload

Active recovery. Daily easy walking or cycling (20–30 min, no breathing effort). Optional light shadowboxing 1–2 times (10 min, no bag, no power — pattern maintenance only). Standard calisthenics deload per CalisthenicsApp. Extra sleep and mobility focus.

Updated Warm-Up Protocol · every bag session · 6 min
  • Chin Tucks
    2×10 reps, slow. Head slides back, gaze level. 15–30 sec between sets.
  • Cervical Rotations
    5 reps each side. Pain-free range only.
  • Lateral Neck Stretch
    20–30 sec each side. Gravity only.
  • Scapular Retractions
    2×15 reps. Mid-back activation, unloads cervical spine.
  • Shoulder Rolls
    10 forward, 10 backward. Releases upper trapezius.
  • Shoulder Depression Drill
    10 reps — actively push shoulders DOWN away from ears, hold 3 sec. Universal prep cue for all punches and hooks.
  • Hip Rotator Mobility — New This Cycle
    10 wide hip circles each side (slow) + 10 lateral hip swings each side + 10 slow mawashi geri in the air each side. Targets the lead hip rotators specifically. Not optional.
  • Movement Prep
    Light shadowboxing (no power) + 10 knee raises each side + 30 sec bounce.
Hip rotator mobility is new this cycle and directly targets the lead mawashi geri weakness. The lead hip rotators are undertrained relative to the rear side — consistent warm-up activation will build both mobility and proprioception over the 6-week cycle.

Atemi-Waza Development — Mesocycle 5 Context

Strikes as setups, not endpoints. In jujitsu, atemi-waza exists to create conditions for throws, joint locks, and takedowns — not as a standalone striking system. From Week 2 this cycle, technique sessions begin framing each combination with its jujitsu purpose: what does this strike set up, and what entry does it create?

The three entry patterns to develop:

1. Posture disruption — jab or cross destabilises balance, creating a kuzushi-like opening for a clinch or takedown entry.

2. Distance creation — mae geri or mawashi geri pushes the opponent to the range where a throw entry or level change becomes available.

3. Closing with control — hook or knee closes into clinch range; the next action is a grip, a throw, or a takedown — not another strike.

These patterns begin in technique sessions. Output sessions condition the striking mechanics — the tactical framing is applied in the slower, more deliberate A sessions.
