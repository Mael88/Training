Mesocycle 6 ran long and felt it — the earlier deload wasn’t enough. You took two full deload weeks this time, and Mesocycle 7 starts from genuinely recovered ground, cervical resolved.

Restart: the original 28 Sep start didn’t happen — about four weeks off the bag on top of the deload, irregular calisthenics, then two weeks out with a cold. The cycle restarts on 2 Nov with a re-entry week (one easy bag session, calisthenics one level easier) before Week 1.

Response: same 2 upper + 2 legs + 2 bag split, which held the cervical through Mesocycle 6. The cycle is built around mawashi geri technique and distance reading — a dedicated ladder of drills, one step per week.

Distance Skill Ladder
WkDrillYou know it’s working when…
1Stand, call, kick. Say “shin” or “foot” out loud, then land on that point of the leg. Same body-height target.The contact point matches the call on most kicks.
2Move, call, kick. Step in or out first, call the distance you ended up at, then kick.Calls stay right after a step, not only from a fixed stance.
3Bag chooses. Let the swinging bag change the range, call it, then kick.You read the distance before the leg is already moving.
4–5Same calls at pace, inside the fatigued rounds.Accuracy holds when breathing is hard.
6Taper: light calls, no chasing reps.Distance reading feels automatic.
Each session logs a simple score: how many of the “blind calls” matched the contact point. That number is the progress metric for the cycle.
Threads For This Cycle

Shin vs Foot Distance — Primary Focus

A distance skill, not a height skill. Shin range is the default and more forgiving; foot range is about half a step further with the ankle extended. Same body-height target for both. Status coming in: “partially” developed.

Lead Mawashi Closing On The Rear

Rear is more accurate than lead, and lead hip quality still logs “moderate”. Lead gets the first and the larger share of reps in every distance drill; hip drive is logged each Output session.

HR Recovery At Matched RPE

A bigger, faster drop at 30 sec after a round signals better conditioning — but only when RPE and temperature match. Chest strap connected every session; ankle-worn sensor data is not reliable enough to compare.

Maintained Habits — Do Not Drop

Controlled rear chamber return, the dynamic hip and adductor warm-up (no held chambers), shoulder-blade depression on every punch, cervical prehab in every warm-up.

Parked — Head-Height Kicking

A real long-term goal tied to hip mobility. Not chased this cycle; revisit once distance reading is automatic.

Phase Details

Re-Entry + Weeks 1–3 — Re-Load & Build

The toolkit is established, so this is a load rebuild plus the distance ladder. The re-entry week and Week 1 are deliberately easy: about six weeks without bag contact plus a cold, and both bag days sit after a legs day. Kick volume returns to full in Week 2; Output climbs into Z4 in Week 3.

Upper body stays at the easier CalisthenicsApp level for the re-entry week, then roughly 2 reps from failure in Weeks 1–2 (no max sets) while the neck and shoulders re-adapt to the upper days. The cap lifts in Week 3 if cervical has logged good throughout. CalisthenicsApp keeps the session content.

Weeks 4–5 — Intensification

RPE and HR targets step up and the distance calls are tested under real fatigue. If Week 4 finishes clean (cervical good, RPE under 9), Week 5 Output tightens rest from 60 to 45 sec. HR recovery gets compared at matched RPE and temperature.

Week 6 — Taper

Volume drops about 30%, intensity holds. The goal is a fresh arrival at the deload — with sleep, cervical status and HR trends deciding whether the full 2-week deload is needed.
