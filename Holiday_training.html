Both Weeks · Daily

Hip Exercises

Lead hip rotators · No equipment · Beach, trail or room
These four exercises target the lead hip external rotators and abductors — the muscles that drive the lead mawashi geri. Done daily across both holiday weeks, the accumulated volume will produce meaningful early-stage strength adaptation before returning to the bag.
Rest protocol: 30 sec between sets of the same exercise · 60 sec between exercises
Lead hip first — always
For every exercise, start with the lead leg. The lead side is the weaker side — it gets the first rep when the muscles are freshest.
1
Clamshells
2 × 15 each side
Setup
Lie on your side. Hips stacked, knees bent at 90°, feet together.
Movement
Keeping the feet together, rotate the top knee upward as high as you can without the top hip rolling backward. Hold 1 sec at the top, lower with control.
Feel: burn in the outer glute and hip. If you feel it in the lower back, the hip is rolling — reduce the range.
Common error: pelvis tilts backward as the knee rises. Keep hips stacked and the movement in the hip joint only.
30 sec between sets · 60 sec before next exercise
2
Side-Lying Hip Abduction
2 × 12 each side
Setup
Lie on your side. Bottom knee slightly bent. Top leg fully straight, toes pointing forward not toward the ceiling.
Movement
Lift the top leg to 45°, then lower with control. 3 seconds on the way down — the eccentric phase is where the strength adaptation happens. Do not let the leg drop.
Common error: toes rotate toward the ceiling, turning it into a hip flexor exercise. Keep toes pointing forward throughout.
30 sec between sets · 60 sec before next exercise
3
Fire Hydrants
2 × 12 each side
Setup
On all fours. Wrists under shoulders, knees under hips. Spine neutral, core lightly engaged.
Movement
Keeping the knee bent at 90°, lift one knee out to the side to hip height. Hold 1 sec at the top, return with control. The rest of the body stays completely still.
Why this matters: this is the same hip joint action as the mawashi geri — external rotation with abduction. You are training the exact muscles that drive the roundhouse kick.
Common error: pelvis tilts to the opposite side as the leg lifts. If the body tilts, reduce the range of motion.
30 sec between sets · 60 sec before next exercise
4
Standing Hip Circles
2 × 10 each side · both directions
Setup
Stand on one leg. Light touch on a wall or tree for balance if needed. Raise the opposite knee to hip height in front.
Movement
Draw large, slow circles with the raised knee — forward, out to the side, behind, and back to front. 10 reps clockwise then 10 counter-clockwise on the same leg before switching. The slower the circle, the harder the hip has to work.
30 sec between sets · No rest after — last exercise
Total time & timing
Full block takes 10–12 minutes. Do it in the morning before activity or in the evening. Leave at least 1 hour between hip exercises and swimming or running in either direction.
Week 1 · Beach · 3–4× per week

Swimming Sessions

Open water · Z2 base + one Z3 session per week
Two types of session: easy base sessions most days and one harder session per week. Open water does not suit structured intervals — keep it continuous and controlled. Goal is cardiovascular base building, not high-output training.
HR target
Stay below 152 bpm (Z3 ceiling) for easy sessions. If breathing so hard that conversation is impossible, you are going too hard. Use Garmin to check occasionally but do not obsess over it.
○
Easy Base Session
20–25 min
2–3 times per week · Z2 · Conversational pace
Warm-up — 3 min
Very easy entry. Light movement, get comfortable in the water.
Main — 18–20 min continuous
Steady pace where you could speak in short sentences. Any stroke comfortable for open water. No sprinting, no stopping to rest.
Cool-down — 2 min
Slow down progressively. Float on your back for 1 minute at the end if conditions allow.
●
Harder Session
25–30 min
Once per week only · Z3 intervals · RPE 7
Warm-up — 5 min
Easy continuous swimming. Settle in before pushing pace.
Main — 4 rounds
3 min at a hard-but-controlled pace (talking difficult but not sprinting) → 2 min floating or treading water → repeat 4 times.
Cool-down — 5 min
Easy swimming back to resting pace. HR below 133 before leaving the water. Float on back 1–2 min at the end.
Safety — open water
Never swim alone. Stay within standing depth until warmed up. Be aware of currents. If conditions are unsuitable, skip the session.
Week 2 · Mountains · 3–4× per week

Running Sessions

Trail running · Z2 base + one Z3 session per week
Same cardiovascular structure as the swimming week — easy base sessions most days, one harder session per week. Mountain trails are cervical-friendly compared to pavement because the softer uneven ground absorbs impact rather than transmitting it up the spine. Stick to paths and trails, not road.
Altitude — important
If you are above ~1500m, your HR will run higher than usual at the same effort because the heart works harder in thinner air. Use RPE as your primary guide this week, not HR targets. Easy should feel easy, regardless of what the watch shows. The HR numbers will normalise when you return to lower altitude.
Running warm-up — mandatory
Always start every run session with 5 minutes of easy walking before breaking into a jog. Cold muscles on mountain trails increase the risk of ankle rolls and strains. The cervical prehab routine before running is also recommended — it only takes 5 minutes and primes the neck for the impact.
○
Easy Base Session
20–25 min
2–3 times per week · RPE 5–6 · Conversational pace
Walk warm-up — 5 min
Easy walking before any running. Essential on mountain terrain. Primes ankles and calves.
Main — 15–20 min easy jog
Steady pace where you could hold a conversation. On trails, slow down significantly for technical sections or uphill — hiking uphill and jogging flat or downhill is perfectly valid. Total time is the goal, not continuous running. Keep RPE 5–6 throughout.
Walk cool-down — 3–5 min
Slow down to a walk for the last 3–5 minutes. Do not stop abruptly. Light lower body stretching after — calf and hip flexor stretch, 30 sec each side.
On hills: running uphill significantly raises HR and RPE. Walk all significant uphills and jog the flat and downhill sections. This keeps the effort in the right zone and is also easier on the knees and cervical spine.
●
Harder Session
25–30 min
Once per week only · RPE 7 intervals · Use RPE not HR at altitude
Walk warm-up — 5 min
Easy walking before any running. Do not skip at altitude.
Easy jog — 5 min
Settle into a comfortable jog before the intervals begin.
Main — 4 rounds
3 min at RPE 7 (harder pace, talking becomes difficult) → 2 min easy walk or very slow jog to recover → repeat 4 times. On mountain trails, a flat or gently downhill section is ideal for the effort intervals. Do not attempt RPE 7 intervals on steep uphill terrain.
Walk cool-down — 5 min
Easy walk back to full rest. Calf stretch 30 sec each side, hip flexor lunge 45 sec each side after stopping.
Altitude note: at altitude, RPE 7 may correspond to a much lower actual pace than at sea level. That is fine — the physiological demand is the same. Do not try to match your usual sea-level running pace.
Trail safety
Watch your footing on uneven terrain, especially on descents where ankle rolls are most likely. Reduce pace significantly on technical sections. If a path is too steep or technical to run safely, walk it. The session goal is cardiovascular stimulus, not trail running performance.
