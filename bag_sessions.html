Week 3 Debrief
RPE Cap — Heat Enforcement
RPE 9 at 33°C in Session B, second week running past the intended ceiling. The cap exists specifically because heat and maximal effort together carry real risk — not just a training inefficiency. This week the structure enforces it directly: if RPE reaches 9 before the session ends, remaining rounds automatically drop to recovery pace rather than staying at full output. See the hard-stop rule in Session B.
What Worked
Pause drill effective — distance corrected after the pause in Session A, and held through full output in Session B. Lead vs rear mawashi now clearly distinguishable. Lead mawashi feels stronger and more stable than Week 1 — the hip work is showing early results. Tactical entry choice felt deliberate in the free rounds.
Clarification — Hook + Knee, Strike + Clinch Simultaneity
Landing the hook and knee almost together, or striking while gripping, is correct execution, not an error. The pause drill exists only for kicks — long weapons that need room to extend. Hooks and knees are close-range weapons that work because you are already at the right distance. There is no distance to lose there. Flowing the hook directly into the knee, or striking while gripping in a clinch entry, is genuine advanced atemi-waza. This week, one round is dedicated to practising this flow deliberately.
Intensification A — Cardio Priority
Breathing/cardio has been the top fatigue signal for three consecutive weeks. This phase is built to address it directly — slightly reduced rest in Session B, HR recovery tracking during rest periods, and a deliberate push on aerobic capacity within the RPE/heat safety limits.
No new strikes this cycle — deepening what exists. Today: consolidate the four established combinations, and dedicate one round to the hook–knee flow and clinch-strike concept explicitly, since it came up naturally in your last session.

2
Hook → Knee Flow — No Pause
NEW FOCUS
Clinch-Range Weapon Flow
This is the opposite instruction to the kick pause drill. Lead hook → rear hiza geri, thrown as close to simultaneous as you can manage — no gap, no distance check. You are already at the correct range for both weapons. The hook lands, the knee drives up almost on top of it.
Why no pause here: kicks need room to extend, so punches rushing in afterward create a distance problem. Hooks and knees are both close-range — there is no room to lose. Slowing down here would actually be incorrect; the flow between them should be tight.
Grip variation: for half the reps, grip the bag briefly with the lead hand as the hook lands, then drive the knee while holding the grip. This simulates striking while controlling in a clinch — a real and valid jujitsu application, not a mistake.
Lead hook → rear hiza geri (near-simultaneous) → release → step back → reset → repeat.
3
Rear Mawashi + Cross-Lead Hook + Rear Hiza Geri
SHARPEST
Your Most Natural Combination
Rear mawashi geri → pause → step off swing line → cross-lead hook → rear hiza geri. Pause drill still active on the kick (rear mawashi needs it) — then no pause between the hook and the knee, flow them together as in Round 2.
Push toward 70–75% effort. This combination is solid — use it to build confidence into the harder combinations later in the round.
Rear mawashi → pause → step off line → cross-lead hook → rear hiza geri (flow, no pause) → step back → reset.

FOCUS
Your Least Natural Combo Under Fatigue — Priority Work
This was the combination that held up worst under fatigue last week. Extra repetitions here, at a controlled pace.
Break it into two halves if needed: lead mawashi → pause → jab-cross first, get that clean, then add the rear hiza geri once the entry feels solid. Rebuild it in pieces rather than forcing the full sequence if it feels shaky.
Pause drill active after the lead mawashi. No pause needed between the cross and the knee — flow them.
Lead mawashi → pause → step in → jab-cross → rear hiza geri (flow) → step back → reset.

Established Atemi Setup
Lead mae geri → pause → step in → jab-cross-lead hook. Well established — use this round to deepen the tactical feel rather than the mechanics.
As the hook lands, feel the proximity it creates. This week, add one thing: after the hook, hold the clinch position for one full second before stepping back — feel what it would mean to control from there, rather than immediately disengaging.
Mae geri → pause → step in → jab-cross-lead hook → hold 1 sec → step back → reset.
6
Free Round — Choose Entry, Flow the Finish
Tactical Free Round
No prescribed combination. Choose your entry deliberately each time. RPE 6–7 ceiling.
This week’s addition: whichever entry you choose, if it ends in a hook or a knee, flow the finish without a pause. If it involves a kick opening the sequence, keep the pause after the kick lands.

Mistakes Reference

Review during rest periods
New this week: the distinction between pause (kicks) and flow (hooks/knees). Getting this distinction right is the main technical goal.
Pause vs Flow — The Core Distinction
SituationRuleWhy
After any kick (mae geri, mawashi geri)Pause 1 sec, check distance, then punchKicks need room to extend. Punches rushing in afterward create a distance problem.
After a hook, before a kneeNo pause — flow togetherBoth are close-range weapons. You are already at the right distance. Slowing down is incorrect here.
Grip + strike (clinch simulation)Simultaneous is correctReal jujitsu clinch application — striking while controlling, not a sequencing error.
Heat & RPE Safety Rules
  • Hard stop: if RPE hits 9 before the session ends, remaining rounds drop to recovery pace. This is not optional.
  • Heat rest extension: 75 sec rest instead of 60 if temperature is 30°C or above.
  • Hydration: drink before starting and between every round in heat, not just when thirsty.
