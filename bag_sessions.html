Week 2 Debrief
Rear Mawashi Recovery — Resolved
Held throughout both the fresh round and the fatigued round. Three weeks of dedicated isolated work paid off — this graduates from an active correction to a maintained habit. No dedicated isolated round for it this week; the cue stays present in combinations, but the drilling stops.
New Focus — Supporting Leg Stability
Sharp self-diagnosis: if the supporting leg isn’t stable and actively engaged, the hip cannot fully rotate into the kick regardless of how correct the striking leg’s mechanics are. This is a different muscle group than the recovery fix — calf, ankle, and single-leg stability under load, not hip flexor endurance. New dedicated round today, plus new warm-up work below.
Rebuild B — Full Kick Volume Returns
Legs have felt fresh for two weeks straight on the new Tue/Fri frequency. Kick volume moderation lifts starting today. RPE nudges up slightly to match — Session A 6–7, Session B 7–8.
Cervical Watch — Mild in Session B
Not a flare, and not alarming on its own, but worth watching closely given the entire reason for the split change was protecting the cervical spine. If it shows up again this week, flag it immediately.
Hip exercises moved into the bag warm-up. The separate 3×/week block wasn’t happening — that’s a scheduling problem, not a motivation problem, so it moves into the session you’re already completing every time. Condensed to what matters most: the new supporting-leg work plus one hip rotator strength exercise carried over.
Warm-Up

Cervical Prehab 5 min

  • Chin tucks — 2×10, head slides back, gaze level
  • Cervical rotations — 5 reps each side, pain-free only
  • Lateral neck stretch — 20–30 sec each side, gravity only
  • Scapular retractions — 2×15
  • Shoulder rolls — 10 each direction
  • Shoulder depression drill — 10 reps, hold 3 sec

Hip & Supporting-Leg Strength 4–5 min

  • 10 wide hip circles each side
  • New: 10 single-leg calf raises each side — slow, full range, controlled. Builds the ankle and calf stability your supporting leg needs during the kick.
  • New: 20 sec single-leg balance hold each side, slight knee bend, arms out if needed for balance. Directly simulates standing on one leg while the other leg is doing work.
  • 8–10 fire hydrants each side — same hip rotator action as the mawashi geri, carried over from the previous protocol

Movement Prep 2 min

  • Light shadowboxing — all weapons, no power
  • 10 knee raises each side
  • 10 slow mawashi geri in the air each side — focus on the supporting foot pressing into the ground
  • 30 sec light bounce

1
Full Punch Toolkit

2
Supporting Leg Engagement — Both Mawashi Geri
NEW FOCUS
Root Through the Supporting Foot
Lead and rear mawashi geri, isolated, slow tempo. New cue: as the hip rotates, actively press the supporting foot into the ground — like screwing it into the floor. This is what gives the hip a stable base to rotate against. A passive or unstable supporting leg limits how hard you can commit to the kick, regardless of the striking leg's technique.
Self-check: does the supporting knee feel stable through the rotation, or does it feel like it's giving way slightly? If it's giving way, slow down further and focus purely on the press-into-the-floor sensation before adding any speed back.
This directly targets what you noticed last week — the strength ceiling of the supporting leg limiting rotational effectiveness.
Lead mawashi (root through support foot) → reset → rear mawashi (root through support foot) → reset → repeat.
3
Rear Mawashi + Cross-Lead Hook + Rear Hiza Geri
Sharpest combination, back to full effort. Rear mawashi (recovery now automatic, supporting foot rooted) → pause → step off swing line → cross-lead hook → rear hiza geri (flow). 70% effort.
Rear mawashi → pause → step off line → cross-lead hook → rear hiza geri (flow) → step back → reset.
4
Full Kick Round — Mae Geri + Both Mawashi
Full kick volume resumes — all three kicks, both legs, no restriction. 65–70% effort. Carry the supporting-foot cue into every mawashi rep.
