Lead Mawashi Geri — Distance Drill
CORRECTION
Fixing the Distance Problem
Lead mawashi geri in isolation this round. This round directly addresses ending up too close after the kick.
Distance reset step — mandatory for lead mawashi: same rule as the rear version. Before launching the lead mawashi geri after punches, a reset step back is required to create the correct kicking distance. The kick must reach the bag comfortably from an extended but not over-stretched position.
Sequence for this round: throw 2 jabs (which close distance), then consciously step back before the lead mawashi. Repeat until the step-back-before-kick pattern feels automatic.
Hip drive cue: drive the hip forward and laterally into the kick. Torso stays upright. Supporting foot pivots. Same cue as always — the holiday work should make this feel more available.
If you still end up too close after the reset step, you are not stepping far enough. The kick should feel easy to reach, not stretched.
Jab–jab → reset step back → lead mawashi geri → reset stance → repeat.
4
Mae Geri → Jab-Cross — Posture Disruption
SETUP
Atemi Setup — Posture Disruption
Jujitsu intent: the front kick drives the opponent backward and breaks their posture. Their weight shifts to the rear leg, their structure opens. The step in with jab-cross enters that disrupted window. This is the kuzushi principle at striking range — you create the opening, then you enter it.
Let the intention be present without forcing it. If it clicks, note it. If it does not click this round, it will come. The combinations themselves are correct — the tactical frame is a layer that develops over several weeks.
Mae geri → step in → jab-cross → step back → reset → repeat.
5
Lead Mawashi + Jab-Cross — Distance Corrected
LEAD KICK
Lead Roundhouse Entry — With Reset Step
Lead mawashi geri → step in → jab-cross. The problem from Week 1 was ending too close after the kick. The reset step on the punch side (step back before the kick in Round 3) is now applied to the kick-then-punch direction too.
The sequence: lead kick lands → the bag swings slightly → step to the side of the swing arc → step in for punches. The step-to-the-side naturally creates correct punching distance after a kick.
RPE 6 — mechanics focus. Power comes with time and hip strength.
Lead mawashi → step off swing line → step in → jab-cross → step back → reset.

Mesocycle 5 · Week 2 · Reference

Mistakes Reference

Review during rest periods
This week’s priority correction: distance reset step before lead mawashi geri. The same rule that applies to the rear mawashi now applies to the lead. All other corrections carry forward.
Lead Mawashi Distance — This Week’s Correction
  • Error: launching the lead mawashi geri after punches without stepping back first — ending too close for the kick to land properly.
  • Cause: punches naturally close distance. The same reset step required before the rear mawashi is equally required before the lead.
  • Fix: after any punch sequence, take a deliberate step back before launching the lead mawashi geri. The kick should reach the bag comfortably, not at full stretch.
  • After the kick: step to the side of the bag’s swing arc, then step in for punches. This also manages the distance on the other side of the kick.
Hip Strength Progression — Reminder
  • Warm-up mobility (hip circles, lateral swings, air mawashi) → builds range of motion and proprioception
  • Leg day hip exercises (clamshells, side-lying abduction, fire hydrants) → builds the strength for the hip to drive powerfully
  • Bag repetition → trains the hip to fire under the specific demands of the kick pattern
  • All three together produce the adaptation. None alone is sufficient.
