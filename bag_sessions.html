Week 2 Debrief
Heat Factor — Noted
31–32°C conditions significantly inflate HR and RPE beyond the training load itself. The 179 bpm Session A and RPE 9 Session B are partially heat-driven, not purely fitness. Hydrate thoroughly before and during sessions. If the heat continues this week, trust RPE over HR and do not chase Z3–Z4 HR targets if the conditions are extreme.
What Worked
Atemi intent clicked clearly in Session A — meaningful progress. Lead mawashi felt more natural after repetition within the session. Rounds 3, 4 and 6 combinations flowing naturally. Self-diagnosis on the distance-after-kicks problem is accurate and shows good body awareness.
Distance After Kicks — Pause Drill Added
The problem is identified: rushing into punches without checking distance. This week, a deliberate pause cue is built into kick–punch combinations. The pause is temporary — it trains the check until it becomes instinctive, then disappears. Every combination that starts with a kick this week includes an explicit “land → pause → check distance → punch” instruction.
Hip Exercises — Week 1 of Home Protocol
Hip exercises now running on Monday, Wednesday, Saturday calisthenics sessions. First week of home protocol. No improvement expected yet — it takes 2–3 weeks for early adaptation to show. Note any subtle changes in the post-session log if you feel them.
Accumulation B begins. Combinations get longer and the atemi setup framing gets more explicit. From this session, every round has a named tactical purpose — not just what to throw, but why each strike exists in the sequence and what it creates.

1
Full Punch Toolkit — Shoulder Blade Check
Foundation
All punches at 65% effort. Include jab-lead hook and cross-rear hook. Shoulder blade active on every cross and hook — confirm it is still automatic.
Longer combinations this week: aim for 3–4 strike sequences rather than 2-strike combos. The individual strikes are solid — now chain them together more fluidly.
2
Lead Mawashi Geri — Pause & Check Drill
LEAD KICK
Distance Check Training
Lead mawashi geri → land → deliberate 1-second pause → check your distance to the bag → then punch. This round is about training the distance check as a habit.
Why the pause: the instinct to rush punches after a kick is strong and automatic after two mesocycles of combinations. The pause interrupts that rush and forces the check. After several weeks of deliberate pausing, the check becomes fast enough that the pause disappears. You cannot skip this stage.
Lead mawashi hip cue: drive the hip forward and laterally. Torso stays upright. Supporting foot pivots. After two weeks of the warm-up mobility work, does the lead hip feel slightly more available than Week 1?
Lead mawashi → land → 1 sec pause → distance check → step in or adjust → jab-cross → reset.
3
Mae Geri + Jab-Cross-Lead Hook — Kuzushi Entry
SETUP
Atemi Setup — Posture Disruption into Clinch Entry
Jujitsu intent: the front kick disrupts posture and creates a weight-shift reaction in the opponent. The jab-cross enters into that disrupted moment. The lead hook closes the distance into clinch range — after the hook, you are inside. In jujitsu, this is the entry point for a throw, a takedown, or a clinch control. Feel the hook bringing you inside and hold that proximity for one beat before stepping back.
This is a longer combination than Week 2. Front kick → step in → jab → cross → lead hook. Four actions, one connected intention. The kick opens, the punches enter, the hook closes.
Pause drill active: after the mae geri lands, 1-second pause, check distance, then step in for the punches.
Mae geri → pause → step in → jab-cross-lead hook → feel the inside → step back → reset.
4
Rear Mawashi + Cross-Lead Hook + Rear Hiza Geri
SETUP
Atemi Setup — Rotational Disruption into Knee Finish
Jujitsu intent: the rear mawashi geri to the body rotates the opponent’s structure — their torso turns away from the kick, their weight shifts. The cross-lead hook enters into that rotated, off-balance position. The hook closes into clinch range; the rear knee finishes inside. This sequence moves through all three ranges in four strikes.
Rear mawashi only this round — not lead. Your stronger kick opens the combination. After landing, step off the bag’s swing line, then step in for the punches.
Pause drill active: after the mawashi lands, 1-second pause, step off swing line, check distance, then punch.
Rear mawashi → pause → step off swing line → cross-lead hook → rear hiza geri → step back → reset.
5
Lead Mawashi + Jab-Cross + Rear Hiza Geri
LEAD KICK
Lead Kick Full Sequence — Pause Drill
Lead mawashi geri → pause → step off swing line → step in → jab-cross → rear hiza geri. Full three-range entry using the lead kick.
Comparison round: notice whether this feels harder than Round 4 (rear mawashi entry). The lead kick produces less hip power and a slightly different entry angle. Both sequences are valid — the lead version is faster, the rear version is more powerful. In a real application, which one you throw depends on your position relative to the opponent.
Lead mawashi → pause → step off line → step in → jab-cross → rear hiza geri → step back → reset.
6
Free Round — Choose Your Entry
Tactical Choice Round
No prescribed combination. Use the full toolkit. RPE 6 ceiling.
This week’s free round task: before each combination, choose your entry deliberately — will you open with a kick (which one?), a jab, or something else? Make the choice consciously, then execute. This builds tactical awareness: not just executing patterns, but selecting the right one for the distance you are at.
Pause drill applies: whenever a kick opens a combination, include the 1-second distance check before the punches.

Post-Session Log

Coaching Notes

Pause drill: it will feel unnatural at first — a deliberate pause mid-combination is artificial by design. That discomfort is the point. After 2–3 weeks of doing it, the check becomes fast enough that the pause is no longer needed. Do not skip it to make combinations flow better.

Lead vs rear mawashi comparison (Rounds 4 and 5): notice the entry angle and hip feel. The rear entry is more powerful. The lead is faster. Both are useful in different tactical moments. Beginning to feel the difference is the goal this week.

Heat: if conditions are still 30+°C, take slightly longer rest between rounds if needed (up to 2 min) and prioritise hydration. RPE 6 at 32°C is physiologically equivalent to RPE 7–7.5 in normal conditions.

Week 3 · Reference

Mistakes Reference

Review during rest periods
This week’s new element: the pause drill for kick–punch distance management. All other corrections carry forward.
Pause Drill — This Week
  • What it is: a deliberate 1-second pause after every kick lands, before launching punches.
  • Why it exists: trains the distance check that will eventually become automatic. Cannot be skipped to make combinations flow better — the awkwardness is the point.
  • Applies to: every combination that starts with any kick — mae geri, lead mawashi, rear mawashi. Not needed for punch-only or knee entries.
  • When it disappears: after 2–3 weeks of deliberate practice, the check becomes fast enough that the pause is no longer needed. It will be removed from the instructions when the distance problem is resolved under fatigue.
