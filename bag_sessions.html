New This Cycle — Atemi as Jujitsu Setups
From this week, technique sessions begin framing strikes with their jujitsu purpose. The jab is not just a setup punch — it disrupts posture the way a kuzushi disrupts balance. A front kick creates the distance window for a clinch entry. A hook closes range and loads the opponent for a takedown. This framing starts today.

2
Lead Mawashi Geri — Hip Drive Focus
PRIORITY
Lead Mawashi Correction
Lead mawashi geri only this round. Slow and deliberate. This is the primary technical priority of the mesocycle.
The corrective cue: drive the hip forward and laterally into the kick. Do not let the torso tilt back as the leg rises — the hip should be moving toward the target, not away from it. Think of the hip leading the kick, not the leg swinging while the hip stays behind.
Supporting foot pivot: the supporting heel must pivot outward as the hip rotates. If the foot stays flat, the hip is blocked. The pivot enables the hip drive.
Torso check: at the moment of impact, are you upright or tilted back? Upright = correct. Tilted back = hip is not driving. Slow the kick down until you can feel the difference.
Do not compensate by leaning forward either. The goal is neutral torso — upright and stable — with the hip doing the work of delivering power into the kick.
Lead mawashi geri ×3 → pause → torso check → repeat. No rushing.
3
Both Mawashi Geri — Lead vs Rear Comparison
MAWASHI
Comparative Feel
Alternate lead and rear mawashi geri. The goal is to consciously feel the difference in hip drive between the two.
Rear mawashi is your reference: the rear hip fires strongly and you have already felt this working. Use the rear kick as the reference for what correct hip drive feels like — then try to replicate that sensation in the lead version.
The gap between them: the lead will feel weaker. That is expected. The hip rotators on the lead side are undertrained. The goal this week is not to match the rear — it is to begin feeling where the hip engagement is missing and to start recruiting it consciously.
Over 6 weeks of this warm-up protocol and dedicated mawashi work, the lead hip rotators will develop. Do not force power that does not exist yet — correct mechanics now, power follows.
Lead mawashi → reset → rear mawashi → reset → note the difference → repeat.
4
Mae Geri + Jab-Cross — Atemi Setup Framing
SETUP
First Atemi Setup Sequence
Mae geri → step in → jab-cross. The combination you know well from Mesocycle 4 — now with explicit jujitsu framing.
Jujitsu intent: the front kick does not just damage. It pushes the opponent back and disrupts their posture — their weight shifts backward and their guard opens. The step in with jab-cross enters into that disrupted position before they can recover. This is the kuzushi principle applied at striking range.
Feel the sequence tactically: as you deliver the mae geri, visualise the opponent stumbling back slightly. The step in is immediate — you are entering the window created by the kick. The jab-cross lands into an opponent who is off-balance, not set.
RPE 5–6. Keep it deliberate — the tactical framing is the focus, not the output.
Mae geri → step in → jab-cross → step back → reset → repeat.
5
Jab-Cross-Lead Hook — Clinch Entry Setup
SETUP
Hook as Entry Opener
Jab-cross-lead hook. The combination is familiar. The framing is new.
Jujitsu intent: the jab disrupts. The cross creates a reaction — the opponent covers up or moves back. The lead hook comes around the outside of that reaction and closes distance, bringing you into clinch range. After the hook, you are inside. In jujitsu, the next action would be a grip, a throw entry, or a takedown. On the bag, feel that the hook brings you close — that proximity is the point.
After the hook: notice where you are relative to the bag. You should be close — inside kicking range, at knee and clinch range. In a real application, this is where you grip and execute. On the bag, this is where a knee or a clinch grab would follow naturally.
Jab-cross-lead hook → pause and feel the proximity → step back → reset → repeat.

Mistakes Reference

Full toolkit — review during rest periods
This week’s priority error is the lead mawashi geri hip drive. All other corrections are carried forward from Mesocycle 4.
Lead Mawashi Geri — Priority Correction
  • Error: torso tilts back as the lead leg rises. The hip is left behind rather than driving into the kick.
  • Cause: lead hip rotators are undertrained and not yet strong enough to drive powerfully while maintaining upright torso. The body compensates by leaning back.
  • Fix: drive the hip forward and laterally first. The leg follows the hip. Supporting foot pivots outward as the hip rotates. Torso stays neutral — not leaning forward, not tilting back.
  • Self-check: at impact, is the torso upright? If not, the hip is not leading. Slow the kick down until you can feel the hip initiating.
